SHORT REFLECTION

1. What part of the mission was easiest for you?

The easiest part for me was writing the budget_remaining function. 
I understood that I needed to subtract food and transport expenses from the total budget. 
I used the return statement to send the remaining amount back.

2. What test exposed a mistake or gave you confidence that your code was correct?

The normal case, where the budget was 2000, food was 900, and transport was 600, 
gave me confidence because the result was 500. 
The zero-expense and overspend cases also helped me check that the function 
returned 1500 and -300 respectively. These results matched my expected answers.

3. What feedback did you receive?

During my review, I identified that I needed to document my tests more clearly. 
I also needed to test both functions together by passing the result of budget_remaining 
into budget_message. This helped me understand that testing individual functions is useful, 
but checking how they work together is important too.

4. What exactly did you change after the feedback?

I cleaned up the spacing in my function parameters and comments, 
and added a test that passes the calculated remaining balance into budget_message. 
I also recorded each test's inputs, expected result, actual result, and pass/fail status. 
After making changes, I reran the tests to check the results.

5. If you had ten more minutes, what would you check again?

I would check that both functions use the supplied arguments and return the correct 
values without asking for input or printing inside the functions. I would rerun the normal, 
zero-expense, overspend, and message-format tests. I would also confirm that the message 
has the exact wording, spaces, and final full stop.
